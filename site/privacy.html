Privacy Policy for Filler

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Filler fills the free space of a storage device with temporary files and then deletes them again, on an Android phone, tablet or other Android device and on a Wear OS watch. It is published by Serhii Zhyhunenko (SerZhyAle) on Google Play as one app with the identifier com.sza.filler, which covers the device app and the watch app. This policy covers both.

In short:

  • The developer receives none of your data. The developer runs no server, keeps no accounts, and the app contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code.
  • Nothing leaves your device. Filler has no internet permission and sends nothing to anyone.
  • Filler touches only the files it writes. It deletes those temporary files and nothing else; your own files stay as they are.

1. What the app does not do

  • It has no telemetry: no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no advertising identifier and no third-party tracking library. The components it is built from are listed in section 8.
  • It creates no account and asks you to sign in nowhere.
  • It does not open, read, copy or change your own files, and it never deletes a file it did not write.
  • It does not read your contacts, photos, messages, location or the data of other apps.
  • It asks for no Android permission (section 5).

2. What the app reads on your device

  • The folder you choose. You pick a folder on built-in storage or on a memory card in the Android folder picker. Android then lets Filler write into that folder only. Without a choice, Filler uses its own folder on the device, which needs no access grant.
  • Free space. The free and total space of the storage device that holds the folder, to show it to you, to keep a part of it free for the system, and to stop writing before that part.
  • Whether the storage is connected. A memory card can be removed during a run; Filler checks that the storage is there before it writes or deletes.

3. What the app writes and stores

  • Temporary files. Filler writes new files with names it creates itself into the chosen folder, up to the amount you confirm, and deletes them when filling finishes or when you stop it. They contain no data of yours.
  • The list of its temporary files. Before it writes a file, Filler notes the file in a list in its private storage. The list is what lets it delete every one of its files after a stop, a write error, a restart or a removed memory card, and only those files.
  • Settings: the folder you chose and the part of the free space kept for the system.
  • Android backup is switched off for Filler, so none of this is copied to your Google account.

4. What leaves the device

Nothing. Filler declares no internet permission, so it cannot send anything over a network: not to the developer, not to a server, not to another app. The temporary files stay on the storage device you chose until Filler deletes them.

5. Permissions

Filler asks for no Android permission. Access to a folder is something you grant yourself, one folder at a time, through the Android folder picker; Filler's own folder needs no grant. You can withdraw a grant by clearing Filler's storage in the Android settings or by uninstalling it.

6. Google Play

Google Play, the store, may give the developer aggregated statistics such as installs and crash rates from devices whose owners share them with Google. The developer receives no device-level data from the app.

7. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and the developer collects personal data from no one.

8. Third-party components

The app is built from these open-source components: AndroidX, Material Components and the Kotlin libraries. None of them is an analytics, advertising or tracking library.

9. Deleting your data

Uninstalling Filler removes its settings and its list of temporary files. If temporary files of an earlier run are still waiting on a memory card that is not connected when you uninstall, they stay on that card: connect it and let Filler delete them before you uninstall, or delete them yourself in the folder you chose. The developer holds no copy of anything.

10. Open source

Filler is open source software released under the MIT License. The full source code is publicly available at GitHub.

11. Changes to this policy

When the app changes what it reads, writes, stores or sends, this page changes in the same release and the "Last updated" date at the top moves. The text on this page is the text of the version of the app that is currently published.
